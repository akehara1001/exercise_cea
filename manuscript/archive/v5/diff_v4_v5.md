### supplement 块 112（增）
- v5［paragraph］Supplementary Methods S1. Survival model, validation and probabilistic sampling
- v5［paragraph］Reconstructed patient-level data
- v5［paragraph］Kaplan-Meier curves of KEYNOTE-522 were digitized from the published figures: event-free survival by arm and pathological complete response (pCR) status at interim analysis 4, with a data cutoff of 23 March 20214, and overall survival by arm at the final analysis of overall survival, with a data cutoff of 22 March 20245. Patient-level data were reconstructed from the digitized curves and the published numbers at risk with the algorithm of Guyot and colleagues49. A reconstruction was accepted when it reproduced each published milestone estimate within 1 percentage point, each published hazard ratio (HR) within 0.03 and each reported number of events within 2%; the reported numbers of events were not given to the algorithm. The proportions with pCR were those of interim analysis 4 (Supplementary Table S2).
- v5［paragraph］Event-free survival after surgery
- v5［paragraph］Event-free survival was modeled from surgery, set at 7 months after randomization, separately for the four combinations of arm and pCR status. The candidate models were six standard parametric distributions, six Royston-Parmar spline models and three mixture cure models on the relative survival scale, with background mortality from the Japanese life table for women51. A model was eligible if at least 95% of the monthly points with at least 20 patients at risk lay within the pointwise 95% confidence interval of the Kaplan-Meier estimate, if its excess hazard peaked within 3 years after surgery and declined thereafter, and if its all-cause hazard at 10 and 20 years was not below background mortality.
- v5［paragraph］Within each pCR stratum, both arms used the same distribution family. Among the families eligible in both arms whose summed Akaike information criterion was within 4 of the lowest, the family whose 5-year extrapolation was closest to the 5-year event-free survival reported at a later data cutoff was selected43. This gave a Weibull model for patients with pCR and a Weibull mixture cure model for patients without pCR. Event-free survival at 7 years in each stratum was then fixed at the value of the final analysis44 (Supplementary Table S2), by maximizing the likelihood subject to this equality.
- v5［paragraph］Events before surgery, first events after surgery and deaths from adverse events
- v5［paragraph］Before surgery, event-free survival in each stratum followed its Kaplan-Meier estimate, and patients with pCR had no events before surgery. Progression that precluded surgery occurred only in patients without pCR, in the proportion reported for each arm5, with the monthly shape of the Kaplan-Meier estimate before surgery. When the reported proportion exceeded the decline of that estimate, the excess was placed in the first cycle after surgery and taken from the excess hazard, so the total was unchanged.
- v5［paragraph］First events after surgery were divided into locoregional recurrence, including second primary cancers, distant recurrence and death without recurrence, in the proportions of the 2024 report5. Locoregional recurrence progressed to distant recurrence at a constant rate4. Deaths from adverse events, in the numbers reported for each arm4, occurred at a constant hazard during the 12 months of treatment and were moved within the fitted event-free survival without changing it.
- v5［paragraph］Survival after recurrence and background mortality
- v5［paragraph］Overall survival curves of patients with early and late relapse in the Epidemiological Strategy and Medical Economics (ESME) cohort50 were digitized and reconstructed in the same way, and log-logistic distributions were fitted to them. A common shape was used because the shapes of the two groups did not differ significantly. Excess mortality after distant recurrence within 18 months of randomization followed the early-relapse distribution and after later recurrence the late-relapse distribution, in the same way in all arms. Excess mortality after progression that precluded surgery followed the early-relapse distribution.
- v5［paragraph］Survival after locoregional recurrence carried background mortality only. Background mortality came from the 2025 abridged life table for Japanese women51, from age 49 years. The all-cause hazard was never below background mortality; where a fitted model for patients with pCR fell below it late in follow-up, background mortality applied.
- v5［paragraph］Validation
- v5［paragraph］Overall survival was not used to fit or calibrate any parameter. The model was accepted if three criteria, fixed before the comparison, were met. First, overall survival of each arm at years 1 to 5 lay within the pointwise 95% confidence intervals of the reconstructed Kaplan-Meier estimates, or all ten points lay within Bonferroni-adjusted 99.5% intervals. Second, the 5-year difference in restricted mean survival time between arms lay within the bootstrap 95% interval of the Kaplan-Meier difference. Third, overall and event-free survival at 7 years lay within the 95% confidence intervals of the final analysis44. Supplementary Fig. S1 and Supplementary Table S3 show the comparison.
- v5［paragraph］Probabilistic sensitivity analysis
- v5［paragraph］Each of the 1,000 probabilistic samples combined one bootstrap replicate of the survival model with one draw of the other inputs. In each replicate, the reconstructed event-free survival data were resampled within arm together with their pCR status and the models were refitted with the 7-year equality; the 7-year values were drawn from binomial distributions with the stratum sizes of interim analysis 4. The reconstructed ESME data were resampled within group and the log-logistic distributions refitted.
- v5［paragraph］The other inputs were drawn independently from the distributions in Supplementary Table S2: gamma distributions for costs, durations and events per patient, beta distributions for proportions and utilities, and lognormal distributions for hazard ratios, with parameters derived from the 95% ranges. Drug prices and rates based on zero events were fixed, and the duration of the utility decrement for adverse events and the discount rate were not varied. Every sample can be reproduced from its random seed (base seed 20261023, plus the sample number for the bootstrap). The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles across samples give the 95% uncertainty intervals.

### manuscript 块 1（增）
- v5［paragraph］Introduction
- v5［paragraph］Triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) recurs more often than other breast cancers, and its excess risk of distant recurrence is confined to the first 5 years after diagnosis1. After neoadjuvant chemotherapy, patients with TNBC and residual disease have worse survival than those with a pathological complete response (pCR)2. In the KEYNOTE-522 trial of stage II or III TNBC, adding pembrolizumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy and continuing it after surgery increased pCR and prolonged event-free and overall survival3–5. After this regimen, most recurrences still occur within 24 months of randomization6. In Japan, 2,294 of the 3,451 patients who received neoadjuvant chemotherapy for stage II or III TNBC in 2024 also received pembrolizumab7. Pembrolizumab is given in eight doses before surgery and up to nine after3, and one dose costs 398,924 Japanese yen (JPY) at the 2026 list price8.
- v5［paragraph］In the CHALLENGE trial, structured exercise for 3 years after adjuvant chemotherapy for colon cancer prolonged disease-free survival9. Its hazard ratio (HR) for recurrence, new primary cancer or death was 0.72, with a 95% confidence interval (CI) of [0.55, 0.94]9. In breast cancer, randomized trials of exercise during adjuvant chemotherapy found better physical fitness and, in some, less fatigue10–13. An exploratory follow-up of one trial found an HR for disease-free survival of 0.68 (95% CI [0.37, 1.24])14. During neoadjuvant chemotherapy, diet and exercise were associated with a higher pCR in one trial15; supervised exercise did not change tumor size in two others16,17. A meta-analysis of eight trials across cancer types found no difference in pCR18. A trial powered for pCR, with disease-free survival as a secondary outcome, was registered in 202219. None of these trials reported recurrence, so the effect of exercise during neoadjuvant chemotherapy on recurrence is unknown.
- v5［paragraph］The evidence needed to judge exercise during neoadjuvant chemotherapy lacks two things. First, economic evaluations of exercise in cancer have valued effects measured in trials, mostly on fatigue, fitness or quality of life during or after adjuvant treatment20–22. In breast cancer, such evaluations of exercise during adjuvant chemotherapy reached different conclusions23–25. When the effect on recurrence is unknown, a cost-effectiveness ratio cannot be estimated, but the highest program cost that remains cost-effective can be calculated for each possible effect26. Second, cost-effectiveness analyses of the KEYNOTE-522 regimen came from the United States, Hong Kong, Switzerland and Egypt27–30. The Japanese analyses identified concerned pembrolizumab for advanced cancers, including metastatic TNBC31–37. As of October 2026, pembrolizumab had not been designated for Japan's cost-effectiveness evaluation38. Without a Japanese estimate for pembrolizumab, there is no reference against which the cost and health gain of exercise can be judged.
- v5［paragraph］This study aimed to estimate, from the perspective of the Japanese public healthcare payer, the maximum cost per patient at which adding a structured exercise program to neoadjuvant chemotherapy with pembrolizumab for stage II or III triple-negative breast cancer would be cost-effective, and how this cost depends on the size and duration of the effect of exercise on recurrence. The secondary aims were to estimate the same cost when exercise is added to neoadjuvant chemotherapy alone, to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of pembrolizumab in Japan as a reference, and to estimate the effect of exercise that would yield the same gain in quality-adjusted life years as pembrolizumab. No economic evaluation of exercise during neoadjuvant therapy for breast cancer was identified in searches of PubMed and Europe PMC covering 2000 to 2026, last run on 26 October 2026. The nearest, Phys-Can, compared two exercise intensities during neoadjuvant or adjuvant treatment of breast, colorectal or prostate cancer39.
- v5［paragraph］Methods
- v5［paragraph］Study background
- v5［paragraph］The decision problem was whether, and up to what cost per patient, a structured exercise program added to neoadjuvant chemotherapy for stage II or III TNBC was cost-effective in Japan. A state-transition model simulated a cohort of women aged 49 years who started neoadjuvant chemotherapy for stage II or III TNBC, with the regimens and outcomes of KEYNOTE-5223. Four arms were modeled: chemotherapy alone, as in the placebo arm of the trial; chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab; and each of these with exercise added. The main comparison was exercise added to chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab against chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab, and the secondary comparison was exercise added to chemotherapy alone against chemotherapy alone. Both treatments to which exercise was added were in use in Japan in 20247. Chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab against chemotherapy alone formed the reference comparison.
- v5［paragraph］The analysis took the perspective of the Japanese public healthcare payer and a lifetime horizon, to capture differences in survival. Costs and quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) were discounted at 2% per year, as the Japanese guideline for cost-effectiveness evaluation specifies40. The willingness-to-pay threshold for exercise was JPY 5 million per QALY41,42. In Japan's cost-effectiveness evaluation, the higher threshold of JPY 7.5 million per QALY applies only to designated drugs and devices given special consideration, such as those for malignant tumors42. An exercise program is not such a product, so its maximum affordable cost was judged at JPY 5 million per QALY. Pembrolizumab was also assessed at JPY 7.5 million per QALY.
- v5［paragraph］Trial inputs came from KEYNOTE-522 reports with data cutoffs of 23 March 2021, 23 March 2023, 22 March 2024 and 14 October 20254,5,43,44. Published data were extracted from 21 to 26 October 2026. The analysis plan was written before any economic computation, and each later addition was written before the computation it added; the plan was not registered. The study follows the Consolidated Health Economic Evaluation Reporting Standards 2022 (CHEERS 2022)45; the checklist is in Supplementary Table S1.
- v5［paragraph］Outcome
- v5［paragraph］The primary outcome was the maximum affordable cost of an exercise program per patient. It was the willingness-to-pay threshold multiplied by the incremental QALYs, minus the incremental medical costs other than the program cost, in JPY per patient. Increments compared an arm with exercise with the same treatment without exercise, and a program costing less than this amount would be cost-effective. Medical costs avoided were the incremental medical costs other than the program cost, with the sign reversed.
- v5［paragraph］Two secondary outcomes were recurrence HRs: the HR at which the maximum affordable cost equaled the cost of a program, and the HR at which medical costs avoided equaled it. For pembrolizumab, the secondary outcome was the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio (ICER) against chemotherapy alone. The exercise effect that would gain as many QALYs as pembrolizumab was expressed as a recurrence HR and as an increase in pCR. The one-year net benefit was the maximum affordable cost minus the program cost, summed over the eligible patients treated in Japan in one year.
- v5［paragraph］Variables
- v5［paragraph］The exposure was the effect of exercise on recurrence, expressed as the recurrence HR with exercise. This was the HR for locoregional and distant recurrence after surgery in an arm with exercise, relative to the same treatment without exercise (Table 1). It ranged from 0.70 to 1.00, where 1.00 means no effect, and did not act on deaths without recurrence or on events before surgery. The CHALLENGE estimate of 0.72 served as a reference point9.
- v5［paragraph］In the base case, the HR held for 5 years after randomization, after which the effect halved every year. Alternative durations held it until 1 year after surgery or for life. In a further alternative, exercise continued for 30 months after surgery, the effect held until it stopped, and the cost of continuation counted among medical costs. In an alternative pathway, exercise raised the proportion with pCR by 0 to 10 percentage points and did not change the recurrence HR. Musculoskeletal adverse events of exercise occurred at the rates observed in CHALLENGE9, with their management costs and utility decrements.
- v5［paragraph］Three exercise programs were costed for the 6 months of neoadjuvant chemotherapy. The CHALLENGE-based supervised program used the program cost reported for CHALLENGE46, converted from 2024 Canadian dollars by purchasing power parity47. The fee-schedule-based program and the remote low-cost program were priced from the medical fee schedule48.
- v5［paragraph］Event-free survival after surgery came from parametric models fitted to patient-level data reconstructed from the published curves of KEYNOTE-522 by arm and pCR status4,49. Event-free survival at 7 years in each stratum was fixed at the values of the final analysis44. Excess mortality after distant recurrence came from the French Epidemiological Strategy and Medical Economics (ESME) cohort and was the same in all arms50. It differed between recurrence within 18 months of randomization and later recurrence, and progression that precluded surgery took the early-recurrence values. Background mortality came from the 2025 Japanese abridged life table for women51.
- v5［paragraph］Costs were in JPY at fiscal year 2026 prices. Drug costs came from the national drug price list8, and other costs from the medical fee schedule48, published Japanese estimates and clinical experts. Adverse events of pembrolizumab and chemotherapy came from KEYNOTE-522 and the European Medicines Agency assessment report4,52. Utilities were EuroQol 5-dimension 5-level (EQ-5D-5L) values from KEYNOTE-522 with the German value set29,53. This set was the base case because no report applying a Japanese value set to KEYNOTE-522 data with standard errors was found. Values from Japanese populations were used in a scenario54,55. Table 1 summarizes the inputs, and Supplementary Table S2 lists all of them with their ranges, distributions and sources.
- v5［paragraph］Statistical analysis
- v5［paragraph］Fig. 1 shows the model: 1-month cycles from the start of neoadjuvant therapy to age 100 years, with the cohort split by pCR at surgery in month 7.
- v5［paragraph］(1) To reproduce the trial outcomes, the survival model was built from event-free survival and external survival after recurrence, without using overall survival. Overall survival in KEYNOTE-522 served only to validate the model, against acceptance criteria fixed before each comparison5,44. Supplementary Methods S1 describes the model and the criteria, and Supplementary Fig. S1 and Supplementary Table S3 show the comparison.
- v5［paragraph］(2) To estimate the outcomes, the model was run with base-case inputs at recurrence HRs from 0.70 to 1.00 in steps of 0.025. Increases in pCR ran from 0 to 10 percentage points in steps of 1. The HRs at which the maximum affordable cost or the medical costs avoided equaled a program cost were found numerically between 0.30 and 1.00. The exercise effect matching the QALY gain of pembrolizumab was found the same way, between HRs of 0.30 and 1.00 and pCR increases of 0 to 37 percentage points. The results are in Fig. 2 and Tables 2 and 3.
- v5［paragraph］(3) To reflect uncertainty, each of 1,000 probabilistic samples paired one bootstrap replicate of the survival model with one draw of the cost and utility inputs (Supplementary Methods S1). Costs, durations and events per patient followed gamma distributions, proportions and utilities beta distributions, and HRs lognormal distributions. Drug prices and rates based on zero events were fixed. The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the samples gave the 95% uncertainty interval (UI). The share of samples in which the maximum affordable cost exceeded a program cost, or in which pembrolizumab was cost-effective, gave probabilities.
- v5［paragraph］(4) To find the inputs that move the results, one-way sensitivity analyses set each input to its lower and upper bounds (Fig. 3). They covered the maximum affordable cost in the main comparison at an HR of 0.85 and the ICER of pembrolizumab. When a source gave no range, the range was ±25% of the base value, or the missing bound mirrored the given one. Drug prices varied downward only, to 75% of the list price, and these assumed ranges are marked in the figures.
- v5［paragraph］(5) To examine alternative assumptions, scenario analyses changed one assumption at a time (Table 1). Besides the alternative effect durations, they used utility values scored with the United States and United Kingdom value sets27,28 and those of the Australian reimbursement submission56. Higher mortality after early recurrence with pembrolizumab multiplied excess mortality after early distant recurrence, and after progression during neoadjuvant therapy, by a factor from an Italian cohort57. Longer survival after progression during neoadjuvant therapy took the median survival of a single-center cohort with locoregional progression58. Other scenarios halved the monthly costs of treatment after distant recurrence, used eribulin-based later-line treatment59, or added post-operative capecitabine or olaparib for patients without pCR60–62. Results involving pembrolizumab were reported both in the base case and with higher mortality after early recurrence with pembrolizumab.
- v5［paragraph］(6) To express the result for Japan, the one-year net benefit used the fee-schedule-based program at JPY 5 million per QALY for recurrence HRs of 0.95, 0.90 and 0.85. Patients who received neoadjuvant pembrolizumab in the 2024 national registry took the net benefit of the main comparison, and the other patients that of the secondary comparison7. These values are conditional on the HR and are not expected values.
- v5［paragraph］No statistical tests were performed, so no adjustment for multiple comparisons was needed. All analyses were performed in R version 4.5.1.
