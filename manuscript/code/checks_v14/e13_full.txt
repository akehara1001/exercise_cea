P7 [] Literature searches
P8 [] The statement in the Introduction that no economic evaluation of exercise during neoadjuvant therapy for breast cancer was identified rests on three searches, run on 26 October 2026 in PubMed (title and abstract words and Medical Subject Headings) and Europe PMC (title and abstract words), each limited to publication years 2000 to 2026. The first search combined exercise, neoadjuvant or preoperative treatment, breast cancer and economic evaluation. The second searched cancer of any type with neoadjuvant treatment, in any spelling, or with the phrases during treatment and during oncological treatment. The third searched breast cancer with chemotherapy in any setting. The title and abstract of every record were read. The search strings were as follows.

P15 [] Reconstructed patient-level data
P16 [] Kaplan-Meier curves of KEYNOTE-522 were digitized from the published figures: event-free survival by arm and pathological complete response (pCR) status at interim analysis 4, with a data cutoff of 23 March 2021 [4], and overall survival by arm at the final analysis of overall survival, with a data cutoff of 22 March 2024 [5]. Patient-level data were reconstructed from the digitized curves and the published numbers at risk with the algorithm of Guyot and colleagues [49]. A reconstruction was accepted when it reproduced each published milestone estimate within 1 percentage point, each published hazard ratio (HR) within 0.03 and each reported number of events within 2%; the reported numbers of events were not given to the algorithm. The proportions with pCR were those of interim analysis 4 (Supplementary Table S2).
P17 [] Event-free survival after surgery
P18 [] Event-free survival was modeled from surgery, set at 7 months after randomization, separately for the four combinations of arm and pCR status. The candidate models were six standard parametric distributions, six Royston-Parmar spline models and three mixture cure models on the relative survival scale, with background mortality from the Japanese life table for women [51]. A model was eligible if at least 95% of the monthly points with at least 20 patients at risk lay within the pointwise 95% confidence interval of the Kaplan-Meier estimate, if its excess hazard peaked within 3 years after surgery and declined thereafter, and if its all-cause hazard at 10 and 20 years was not below background mortality.
P19 [] Within each pCR stratum, both arms used the same distribution family. Among the families eligible in both arms whose summed Akaike information criterion was within 4 of the lowest, the family whose 5-year extrapolation was closest to the 5-year event-free survival reported at a later data cutoff was selected [43]. This gave a Weibull model for patients with pCR and a Weibull mixture cure model for patients without pCR. Event-free survival at 7 years in each stratum was then fixed at the value of the final analysis [44] (Supplementary Table S2), by maximizing the likelihood subject to this equality.
P20 [] Events before surgery, first events after surgery and deaths from adverse events
P21 [] Before surgery, event-free survival in each stratum followed its Kaplan-Meier estimate, and patients with pCR had no events before surgery. Progression that precluded surgery occurred only in patients without pCR, in the proportion reported for each arm [5], with the monthly shape of the Kaplan-Meier estimate before surgery. When the reported proportion exceeded the decline of that estimate, the excess was placed in the first cycle after surgery and taken from the excess hazard, so the total was unchanged.
P22 [] First events after surgery were divided into locoregional recurrence, including second primary cancers, distant recurrence and death without recurrence, in the proportions of the 2024 report [5]. Locoregional recurrence progressed to distant recurrence at a constant rate [4]. Deaths from adverse events, in the numbers reported for each arm [4], occurred at a constant hazard during the 12 months of treatment and were moved within the fitted event-free survival without changing it.
P23 [] Survival after recurrence and background mortality
P24 [] Overall survival curves of patients with early and late relapse in the Epidemiological Strategy and Medical Economics (ESME) cohort [50] were digitized and reconstructed in the same way, and log-logistic distributions were fitted to them. A common shape was used because the shapes of the two groups did not differ significantly. Excess mortality after distant recurrence within 18 months of randomization followed the early-relapse distribution and after later recurrence the late-relapse distribution, in the same way in all arms. Excess mortality after progression that precluded surgery followed the early-relapse distribution.
P25 [] Survival after locoregional recurrence carried background mortality only. Background mortality came from the 2025 abridged life table for Japanese women [51], from age 49 years. The all-cause hazard was never below background mortality; where a fitted model for patients with pCR fell below it late in follow-up, background mortality applied.
P26 [] Validation
P27 [] Overall survival was not used to fit or calibrate any parameter. The model was accepted if three criteria, fixed before the comparison, were met. First, overall survival of each arm at years 1 to 5 lay within the pointwise 95% confidence intervals of the reconstructed Kaplan-Meier estimates, or all ten points lay within Bonferroni-adjusted 99.5% intervals. Second, the 5-year difference in restricted mean survival time between arms lay within the bootstrap 95% interval of the Kaplan-Meier difference. Third, overall and event-free survival at 7 years lay within the 95% confidence intervals of the final analysis [44]. Supplementary Fig. S1 and Supplementary Table S3 show the comparison.
P28 [] Probabilistic sensitivity analysis
P29 [] Each of the 1,000 probabilistic samples combined one bootstrap replicate of the survival model with one draw of the other inputs. In each replicate, the reconstructed event-free survival data were resampled within arm together with their pCR status and the models were refitted with the 7-year equality; the 7-year values were drawn from binomial distributions with the stratum sizes of interim analysis 4. The reconstructed ESME data were resampled within group and the log-logistic distributions refitted.
P30 [] The other inputs were drawn independently from the distributions in Supplementary Table S2: gamma distributions for costs, durations and events per patient, beta distributions for proportions and utilities, and lognormal distributions for hazard ratios, with parameters derived from the 95% ranges. Drug prices and rates based on zero events were fixed, and the duration of the utility decrement for adverse events and the discount rate were not varied. Every sample can be reproduced from its random seed (base seed 20261023, plus the sample number for the bootstrap). The 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles across samples give the 95% uncertainty intervals.
