CL: Dear Editor,
CL: We submit our original article, "Exercise versus or in addition to pembrolizumab with neoadjuvant chemotherapy for early-stage triple-negative breast cancer: a model-based cost-effectiveness analysis in Japan," for consideration in Breast Cancer Research and Treatment.
CL: Pembrolizumab added to neoadjuvant chemotherapy is now standard care for stage II or III triple-negative breast cancer in Japan, at a drug cost of about JPY 400,000 per dose. Structured exercise during chemotherapy is cheap and safe, but whether it reduces recurrence is unknown. We built a state-transition model on KEYNOTE-522 and compared the four strategies formed by chemotherapy with or without pembrolizumab and with or without exercise, from the perspective of the Japanese public payer. We then asked how large an effect on recurrence exercise would need to match pembrolizumab, and how much a program added to chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab could cost and remain cost-effective. If exercise lowered the recurrence hazard by 5% or more, both strategies without it were dominated. Exercise would need to cut the recurrence hazard by more than half to match the health gained with pembrolizumab, but an effect the size of that in the CHALLENGE trial would give the same net benefit at the Japanese threshold. Added to pembrolizumab, a program priced from the medical fee schedule became cost-effective once it lowered the recurrence hazard by 1.1%, and its value depended on the size and duration of the effect far more than on its price. To our knowledge, this is the first economic evaluation of exercise during neoadjuvant therapy for breast cancer, and it places a non-drug intervention on the same cost-effectiveness scale as a drug, which Japan's formal evaluation, covering drugs and devices only, has not done.
CL: These findings give trials of exercise during neoadjuvant chemotherapy a benchmark and argue that they should measure recurrence and its durability, not pCR alone. The journal has published the systematic review of economic evaluations of exercise for patients with breast cancer that this work extends, and we believe its readers who design and run breast cancer trials are the audience for this analysis.
