To compare exercise with pembrolizumab, each added to neoadjuvant chemotherapy for stage II or III triple-negative breast cancer, and to estimate the maximum cost at which exercise added to pembrolizumab would be cost-effective in Japan.

A state-transition model based on KEYNOTE-522 simulated chemotherapy with or without pembrolizumab, each with or without exercise. It took the Japanese payer perspective over a lifetime. The exercise effect was a hazard ratio (HR) for recurrence after surgery of 0.70 to 1.00. The maximum affordable cost was the threshold of 5 million Japanese yen (JPY) per quality-adjusted life year (QALY) times the incremental QALYs, minus incremental medical costs other than the program cost.

If exercise lowered the recurrence hazard by 5% or more, both strategies without it were dominated. Pembrolizumab cost JPY 2,053 thousand per QALY; exercise would need an HR of 0.425 to match its QALY gain and 0.722 to match its net benefit. Added to pembrolizumab, exercise had a maximum affordable cost of JPY 2,726 thousand at an HR of 0.85 (95% uncertainty interval [2,223, 3,040]). A program priced from the medical fee schedule became cost-effective at an HR of 0.989, and medical costs avoided paid for it at HRs of 0.958 to 0.914.

In Japan, exercise cannot match the health gained with pembrolizumab, but adding it would be cost-effective if it reduced recurrence even slightly; its value depends on the size and duration of that effect far more than on its price. Trials should measure recurrence and whether the effect lasts.
